This is an implementation handoff, not implemented mechanics or calibration. It follows the received [unseated passive rider](RIDER_CONNECTED.md) and the primary [seated/unseated research](RIDER_SEATED_UNSEATED.md). Keep PR #23 draft. Begin with frictionless compression-only support and explicit separation; active rider choice and saddle friction require later independent evidence.

## Geometry and collision ownership

Use a finite named pelvis proxy rigidly attached to `rider_trunk` and a finite named pad on `frame`. Specify their shapes, dimensions and local transforms, contact regularization and maximum admitted penetration independently of the terrain configuration. Preserve explicit body inertias: adding collision geometry must not silently add rider or chassis mass. Treat illustrative values as uncalibrated, and declare receiving budgets before trials.

The rider trunk and frame are parent/child bodies. Ordinary dynamic collision filtering suppresses that pair. Prefer one explicit named `<contact><pair>` for pelvis/pad, with both new geoms' `contype` and `conaffinity` zero to exclude automatic terrain, tyre and other-body interactions. Do not globally disable parent filtering. Verify the actual native model generates the intended pair and no others, in both geom orders.

This follows the pinned MuJoCo 3.16.0 source at `a892750516336d8358949f4518fcc4fbbd85beae`: [parent-body filtering](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/a892750516336d8358949f4518fcc4fbbd85beae/src/engine/engine_collision_driver.c#L325), [explicit-pair filtering](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/a892750516336d8358949f4518fcc4fbbd85beae/src/engine/engine_collision_driver.c#L577), [predefined-pair merging before dynamic filtering](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/a892750516336d8358949f4518fcc4fbbd85beae/src/engine/engine_collision_driver.c#L684) and [remaining predefined pairs](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/a892750516336d8358949f4518fcc4fbbd85beae/src/engine/engine_collision_driver.c#L827). These paths were read directly; their behavior still needs receiving tests in the vendored runtime.

Define the supported upper-pad contact region and normal directions. A finite shape also has sides/underside: either explicitly model those contacts or reject unsupported ones before accepting the step. Do not create an infinite support plane or hidden weld. Finite pad departure must permit supported separation or produce a named unsupported stop, never invented load. Keep adhesion disabled. Distinguish an inactive/proximity contact from load-bearing contact.

## Route contacts before constraint rows

Current `trail-bike.js` assigns every frictionless/elliptic contact row to terrain normal/tangent work. Its recorder requires every contact to be a wheel flex contact. Both assumptions must change before enabling saddle geometry; classifying by constraint type alone is insufficient because saddle and ground may both be frictionless.

Resolve the two named geom IDs once. After each coherent native forward evaluation, classify current contacts using both geom orders and preserve the existing terrain/flex classification. Rebuild contact-index to fresh `efc_address` ownership each time; contacts and row addresses can disappear or reorder. An excluded contact or negative row address has no solved rows. For a solved dimension-one saddle contact, validate the frictionless row type and that `efc_id` identifies that current contact. Reject unsupported contact types/ownership instead of silently assigning them to ground. Retain sparse/dense and native-memory-growth discipline from [named interface observation](RIDER_INTERFACES.md).

Record the native force on the rider and the opposite force on the bike, their world application point and velocities, signed side/pair work, gap/penetration, support state and the native row-work comparison. Use separately owned, preallocated scratch and historical storage. Keep saddle work separate from hand/foot closure, terrain normal/tangent, brakes and passive damping. Native RNE already includes rigid contacts; do not add their forces twice. Retain only the established omitted-flex correction. Saddle contact is internal: external terrain impulse remains wheel-derived, including in an airborne saddle-only fixture.

## Receiving sequence

1. Declare complete versioned settings and numerical budgets. Historical unseated records must retain saddle-disabled behavior and their existing replay path. Choose a new version or an explicit compatible migration; missing geometry or parameters cannot silently enable support.
2. Verify finite geometry, intended parent/child contact, terrain/tyre isolation, both geom orders, dense/sparse row ownership, row reordering/disappearance and zero-load proximity. Independently compare paired wrenches and row/site power. Test airborne saddle contact produces no external terrain impulse.
3. Receive compression, unloading, separation and recontact on both bikes. Check timestep and contact-regularization sensitivity, energy/momentum and bounded penetration against the declared budgets. A geometrically closed pose is not a solved seated equilibrium. The [load-distribution review](RIDER_LOAD_REVIEW.md) requires a gravity-balanced standing/seated reference before treating this as ordinary rider support; compare signed hand/foot/saddle vertical forces separately from axle loads. Compare a matched locked posture separately from the legacy guide's unrelated mass distribution.
4. Exercise nonfinite loads, excess penetration, unsupported pad-side/underside contact and candidate failures across contact creation/loss, including a command boundary. Restore the complete accepted state, command, support geometry, work and impulse. Check observation-cadence invariance, exact replay and owned outputs.
5. Render the actual finite pad/proxy and physical support state in Trail bike, with both-bike desktop/390/320 px inspection and complete browser/headless comparisons. A selected seated intent must not report support when normal load is zero. Keep rider intent separate from contact state.
