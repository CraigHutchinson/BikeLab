## Runtime contract

Select **Rider · estimated effort + feedback** or **Rider · estimated effort hold control** in Trail bike, then configure rider mass as usual. Scenario JSON carries `active.format = "bikelab-rider-estimate-feedback-0.1"` and `response = {frequencyHz: 4, dampingRatio: 1, compensation: "two-wheel-static"}`. The control record model is `bikelab-trail-bike-controller-0.1`. `compensation: "off"` is an explicit comparison arm. Frequency (0.1–10 Hz) and damping ratio (0–3) are nominal response policy, not measured properties or a claim that every admitted setting is stable. Legacy PD examples and records keep their original model and gains. Explicit kp/kd are rejected in the new format so stale fixed gains cannot silently override the runtime policy.

At every physics tick, the controller reads native segment COMs, joint origins and hand/foot endpoints. Configured native masses and gravity determine the ideal bilateral-support force family. The shared statics kernel chooses the feasible minimum squared active torque, including passive spring preload exactly once. Only the four relative joint torques enter the production force vector; hypothetical endpoint forces are recorded but never applied. Root translation and pitch receive no artificial force or torque.

For each joint, the estimate of rotating subtree inertia is `I = sum(I_body_y + m * distance_to_joint^2)`. With `omega = 2*pi*frequencyHz`, active feedback gains are `kp = I*omega^2` and `kd = 2*dampingRatio*I*omega`. The current state changes I where descendant geometry changes. No 70 kg torque/gain table or mass-ratio correction appears in the runtime calculation. Changing configured mass rederives both gains and gravity effort; changing segment geometry also changes the inertia and moment arms. Explicit torque/speed/power ceilings remain illustrative configured actuator limits, not mass-derived human strength. The static estimate does not reserve feedback headroom; the combined request is bounded by the common drive.

These formulas map the isolated rotational second-order plant described by [Lynch and Park, torque/force control part 1](https://modernrobotics.northwestern.edu/nu-gm-book-resource/11-4-motion-control-with-torque-or-force-inputs-part-1-of-3/). Their [multijoint discussion](https://modernrobotics.northwestern.edu/nu-gm-book-resource/11-4-motion-control-with-torque-or-force-inputs-part-3-of-3/) explains why coupled inertia is configuration-dependent. This implementation estimates active contributions: passive stiffness/damping remain present, and free-base, closed-chain and contact coupling are not cancelled. It does not place exact closed-loop poles or solve full inverse dynamics.

The estimate is eligible only when both wheel flexes have a terrain contact at nonpositive geometric distance. A wheel contact bit is not a solved load, friction-feasibility test or equilibrium certificate. There is no hidden hysteresis, filtered history or support-force threshold. Loss of either contact, disabled compensation, geometric degeneracy or torque infeasibility clears all recorded feedforward and hypothetical support forces; PD feedback continues through the same safety limits. Contact switching can change effort instantly. Large accelerations, one-wheel balance and flight are outside the static-support interpretation. Feedback-only fallback is bounded but is not guaranteed to hold the rider against gravity.

The sampled request is `estimate + kp*error - kd*jointVelocity`. The shared drive applies torque and signed power bounds and guards held effort at the next endpoint. Work remains held applied torque times actual accepted joint displacement. Record fields include inertia, kp/kd, estimate, proportional/damping terms, contact mask/status, hypothetical hand/foot world x/z forces, requested/applied effort and saturation. Observation and rendering never update controller policy. It has no memory: restoring mechanical state and target index recomputes the same effort after a rejected step. Launch-only admission avoids promising continuation semantics before a shared controller/target clock is implemented.
