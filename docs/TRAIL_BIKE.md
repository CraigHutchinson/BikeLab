# Connected trail bike

The Trail bike station receives the spatial wheel model into a single moving hardtail or full-suspension bike, including the existing passive guided rider. It is the connected consumer for controlled traction and subsequent articulated rider work, with the earlier stations retained as diagnostic controls. `dist/trail-bike.js` runs identically in the module worker and `node scripts/trail-bike.mjs [scenario-or-record.json]`. Exit 0 means completion within declared numerical/model limits, 2 means an explicit support stop or rejected launch/assembly, and 1 means invalid input or execution failure.

## Model and configuration

The freely translating frame has world x/z slides and a pitch hinge about +y. A fork slide follows the configured head angle. Full suspension uses the shared single-pivot arm and native spatial shock tendon; the hardtail fixes the rear carrier to the frame. Each carrier has a freely spinning hub, 64 default casing nodes with planar displacement, and native capsule-edge ground contacts. Fork, shock and rider guide retain their progressive compliant buffers and ultimate travel boundaries. Ground contact has a normal component and optional homogeneous Coulomb traction. Forward velocity and spin are initial conditions, not prescribed motion; friction couples their subsequent response. Zero friction retains the exact former normal-only path.

The versioned `bikelab-trail-bike-0.4` record owns a complete `scenario` with `bike`, `wheel`, `motion`, `profile`, `assembly`, `surface` and a `commands` array, plus `contactSupport` describing terrain admission and limitations. Complete 0.1 smooth-ground and 0.2 faceted-ground records remain loadable with zero friction; 0.3 retains its traction. All legacy records recompute into 0.4 with zero controls and cannot contain command schedules. Older records cannot claim new traction or 0.1 sharp-profile admission. Partial overrides are accepted for a new run. Saved records require every configuration field and the terrain profile; replay recomputes physics and does not trust supplied frames. All quantities are SI. Wheel arrays are front then rear. Named native bodies/joints/flexes and compiled address tables determine ownership; standalone-wheel coordinate offsets are never assumed.

| Default partition | Mass | Pitch/spin inertia |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Frame + rider | 82 kg | 8 kg m2, split by mass for the compliant rider |
| Front carrier | 0.5 kg | 0.01 kg m2 |
| Rear carrier | 1.5 kg | 0.02 kg m2 about carrier COM at axle |
| Each rotating hub | 1.5 kg | 0.04 kg m2 |
| Each casing | 1 kg | 0.123936 kg m2 in its undeformed state |

Total default mass is 89 kg. Each wheel's rigid-spin inertia is 0.163936 kg m2: hub inertia plus `tyreMass * ((radius - skin)^2 + skin^2/2)`. Carrier and hub inertia do not include casing inertia a second time. The old lumped wheel inertia is not retained; it was smaller than the new ring contribution. Changing hub/tyre mass changes the total mass explicitly. These quantities are illustrative, not measured production-bike configurations. Wheel radius remains the shared 0.36 m geometry; other sizes and independent front/rear wheel specifications are follow-ups.

Launch configuration supplies frame position/velocity, pitch offset/rate, wheel spin relative to carriers, suspension compression/angle and rider displacement. The initial pitch aligns the axle heights before the authored pitch offset. Rear clearance is relative to the zero-height datum, not a terrain-following placement service. No hidden equilibrium settling or artificial post-launch support is applied. The free-flight control disables buffers, gravity and initial spring compression. Imported/custom terrain still undergoes all initial contact and travel checks.

## Contact and support boundary

Ordered rigid polylines may contain vertical faces and coarse facets. The synthetic controls include a 15 mm polynomial crown, a 40 mm square rock, a 60 mm faceted rock and a 200 mm square obstacle that demonstrates an overload stop. Authored corners remain sharp; no rounding or terrain-following animation is substituted. Native convex terrain prisms expose their actual side faces. Collinear subdivisions are canonicalized before collision construction, so splitting the same authored line does not duplicate support.

Profiles retain coordinate, size and complexity bounds, ordered x and no overhangs or retraced vertical faces. Very skinny valid geometric facets may still be rejected during native convex compilation; that is an explicit calculation error, not substituted geometry. Terrain with a vertical face, slope above 0.25 or tangent change above 0.08 rad is labelled `experimental-sharp-impact`; this is an evidence classification, no longer a blanket rejection. Terrain shape, terrain subdivision, casing resolution and timestep are independent. A genuinely reshaped or rounded rock is a different physical input.

At a face, the normal force follows its outward normal. Tangential force adds along the surface when friction is enabled. At an exposed convex corner, admissible normals span the neighbouring face-normal cone; no unique corner normal exists. Independent recorded-witness checks allow half the admitted overlap plus 10 micrometres for contact midpoint placement, and an explicit 0.005 rad native normal accuracy budget for this MVP. Native distance tolerance is not an angular error bound; tightening it did not monotonically improve corner normals. These are bounded numerical controls, not certification of arbitrary rock profiles or local component stress. See [iteration 17 evidence](ITERATION_17_EVIDENCE.md).

The solver rejects unsupported pitch, ultimate fork/shock/rider travel, wheel proximity, authored-domain exit, rim contact, excessive overlap, ring strain/displacement, self-crossing, unresolved per-step movement, native iteration exhaustion and nonfinite state. A failed step restores position, velocity, warmstart and time to the last accepted state. The event records attempted time and available attempted geometry diagnostics; unevaluated quantities remain null, not stale prior values. No failed endpoint enters the visible trajectory or integrated work/impulse. A rejected initial state produces no fabricated frame.

Abstract hub drive and dry-friction brakes are supported. There is no calibrated tyre grip, chain/crank/freehub or brake hydraulic/thermal model, rider pedalling, pressure-derived casing law, anatomical rider contact, frame/fork obstacle collision, 3D steering/lean, soil deformation, mud, damage or pressure loss. A stopped overlap/rim/travel boundary is not a physical crash or failure result. The near-rim straight hub cast now activates whenever the previous rim gap is within the full step displacement, with a numerical allowance; this follows the 1-Lipschitz distance bound. Typed native state is converted to the cast's required arrays. Discrete casing checks and this circular rim cast do not cover curved hub paths or constitute complete swept-casing collision detection. Physical accuracy and real-time execution remain unqualified.

## Forces, moments and work

Each recorded wheel contains every active contact with full force vector, normal/tangent force and solver-relative slip, the deformed casing polyline, hub pose, ground wrench at the axle, parent-on-wheel bearing wrench, world momentum and COM. Front/rear contact identity follows native flex IDs. A ground resultant is distinct from a bearing load. The renderer uses these recorded casing positions and loads, with a tracking camera on narrow displays and a route-wide desktop view; bike tubes illustrate shared joint geometry and the orange rider marker denotes a guided mass, not an anatomical body.

Node slide forces project from hub-local coordinates to world x/z. Hub-spin generalized constraint force minus the solved brake-row torque gives ground My about that hub. The free-root travel/heave/pitch components provide an independent whole-bike ground wrench, transported to world origin for the impulse ledger. Independent contact-list sums verify both attribution and moment transport.

MuJoCo 3.16 postconstraint RNE skips flex contacts. At the same origin, the aggregate parent-on-subtree correction is `rawRNE - omittedFlexGroundWrench`. It is used only for complete wheels and ancestors containing complete wheels. Internal ring-edge and foundation actions cancel within those cuts; this does not establish individual casing/spoke stresses. Native tendon forces are already represented. The additional generalized shock-buffer force is removed through its equivalent shock-eye force when isolating rear-pivot bearing loads. A full-suspension pivot has free y rotation; a hardtail's fixed attachment can carry My.

The diagnostic RNE pass owns separate native data, receives the same state/warmstart/applied forces, and is checked against the integration data's acceleration and constraint force. Observation cadence must not alter the integration trajectory. World body velocities are evaluated at inertial COMs, including inherited frame/arm rotation. The planar angular term uses Iy; future 3D dynamics require the rotated inertia tensor.

Native energy already includes gravity, kinetic energy, tyre foundation/edges, fork, shock and rider springs. Add only custom buffer potential. Native damping work already includes suspension, rider and casing damping; add only custom buffer loss. The balance is `E - E_initial + dampingLoss - signedConstraintWork - driveWork`. Stationary ground contributes no prescribed-support work. Soft-contact constraint work can store/release energy and is not labeled entirely as dissipation. Total ground impulse and gravity moment are integrated at every accepted physics step; output samples are for inspection and require separate quadrature refinement if used to reconstruct impulses.

Root/fork/rear wrenches are parent-on-subtree at the corresponding body origin. The compliant rider guide wrench is at the rider COM; for fixed rider there is no separate rider body/reaction and the field is the near-zero free-root control. Exported momentum and impulse use the world origin. Maximum sampled root/bearing residuals, native full-cone excess and lateral contact force are explicitly sampled diagnostics, not maxima over every integration step. The normal/tangent/brake power-partition residual is checked at every accepted integration step.

## Scheduled hub drive and brakes

New-run defaults contain one zero command. Saved 0.4 records require an explicit array of 1–64 complete snapshots `{tick, rearDrive, frontBrake, rearBrake}`. Ticks are safe integers from zero through the last integration tick, strictly increasing, with the first at zero. Rear drive is signed torque within ±120 N m; front/rear brake commands are nonnegative torque capacities within 0–200 N m. These admission bounds are experimental, not component ratings. Positive drive acts about +y and rolls the rear wheel forward on flat ground.

The editor displays event times in seconds and converts them to integer ticks using the selected timestep. Times must align; it does not round an off-grid user command into a different event. Every row replaces all three controls. Changing the timestep through the editor preserves the entered times when they remain aligned; JSON schedules explicitly own ticks, so changing only `motion.dt` changes their physical times. Presets apply at 0.05 s and release at 0.2 s over a 0.3 s run. Add/remove, restore, JSON import and replay use the same schedule validation.

Commands are right-continuous. An interval advances and integrates endpoint forces with its old command; only then is the boundary command applied and its constraints rebuilt. Command boundaries are recorded even between regular observation samples. A command at the final tick changes endpoint diagnostics but contributes no drive/brake work over the completed interval. If the interval or boundary solve fails, restore positions, velocities, warmstart, time, command and native brake capacities together; the rejected work and impulse never enter the ledger.

Rear drive acts on the native relative hub coordinate, with an equal/opposite carrier reaction. Drive work is torque times **relative hub-angle displacement**, not world spin. Native friction-loss rows provide solved brake torques bounded by the requested capacities. Rows are found by DOF after every solve; their indices are not persistent across capacity changes. Brake power is solved torque times relative spin velocity. Signed brake work is already part of total constraint work and is not counted again as damping loss.

Each wheel exports relative `spinAngle`/`spinVelocity`, drive torque, brake capacity and solved brake torque. `carrier` is the total parent-on-wheel wrench; `bearing` excludes the drive/brake moment and is the ideal hinge-bearing wrench. Ground moment excludes brake torque. Do not remove internal hub torques a second time from root/fork/rear-carrier subtree loads. The renderer offers signed spin and solved brake-torque histories alongside slip and ground loads.

Brakes use the pinned native DOF regularization (`solref=[0.02,1]`, `solimp=[0.9,0.95,0.001,0.5,2]`), currently not separately configurable. Capacity is not guaranteed holding torque: regularized constraints may creep or show small positive instantaneous power. No exact wheel-lock or calibrated brake-response claim follows. See [iteration 19 review](ITERATION_19_REVIEW.md) for primary sources and independent rotor/carrier controls. Detailed drivetrain, rider actuation, thermal and material mechanisms remain follow-ups.

## Homogeneous traction

The surface contains one dimensionless interface coefficient: `surface.friction` is zero (default), or 0.0001 through 2. Positive values set native elliptic condim=3 on both casing flexes and all terrain slabs; zero retains condim=1 and the original solver options. The same coefficient on both sides avoids accidental native maximum mixing. These are illustrative values, not labels for mud, rock or rubber. No rolling/torsional resistance is added. Coast and zero-launch-spin sliding examples both have freely rotating wheels; neither is a locked-wheel brake experiment.

Contact forces transform all three native force components into world coordinates. Signed longitudinal slip is the native relative point velocity projected on the planar tangent (normalZ, -normalX), positive toward +x on flat ground. It is measured in m/s, with no singular low-speed ratio. The new point-velocity diagnostics are provided for traction contacts; zero-friction records use null for these fields; no synthetic rigid-circle slip is substituted. Contact-free samples plot zero, and the viewer's aggregate is normal-load-weighted, not a new tyre constitutive quantity.

Normal and tangent work integrate their native force-times-constraint-velocity components at every accepted step. Together with brake work, their sum reconciles with total generalized constraint work; the energy residual subtracts that total only once. Tangential work is signed, including any small positive regularized samples; it is not clamped to imply perfect passivity. The surface is stationary. Soft contact may store/release energy, and regularization permits creep under sustained tangential load. See [iteration 18 review](ITERATION_18_REVIEW.md) for source conventions, drive/brake ownership and retained material/fidelity work.
