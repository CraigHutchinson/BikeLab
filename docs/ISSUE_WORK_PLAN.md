## New contact feedback

### #48 — Solver compliance versus tyre material

[Issue #48](https://github.com/CraigHutchinson/BikeLab/issues/48) correctly distinguishes contact regularization from a declared tread law. Current `moving-wheel.js` creates `solref=[contactTime,1]` and `solimp=[d,d,.001]`; trail-bike and fixed-hub reuse them. The equal impedance endpoints make the width irrelevant to impedance variation: it is not tread thickness. Casing foundation/edge elasticity is a separate model term. This branch's moving-wheel and trail-bike documentation makes this explicit; the new runtime/browser gates remain separately tracked.

For an isolated constant-impedance normal row, the [official scalar approximation](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/3.3.7/modeling.html#solver-parameters) gives `r_ddot + 2*r_dot/tau + r/(tau²*zeta²) = (1-d)*a_unforced`. With the [pinned regularizer](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/a892750516336d8358949f4518fcc4fbbd85beae/src/engine/engine_core_constraint.c#L1763), `K_static = 1/[A_approx*(1-d)*tau²*zeta²]`. Thus the isolated-row static/transient coefficient ratio is 100 at d=0.99. `A_approx` is the native diagonal approximation, not universally the exact coupled inverse effective mass. The port's 2.3e6 N/m per contact and trajectory/force percentages remain unverified locally; whole-wheel critical damping does not follow.

**Next gate:** receive port fixtures/build settings and test a loaded isolated normal row across d and tau, separating settled penetration from transient response and comparing compiled native coefficients. A tread compliance must be a separately identified material model, with independent energy/storage/loss ownership and calibration. Merely altering solref or interpreting permitted overlap as a tyre property is not that implementation. Documentation addresses the immediate interpretation gap; the calibration/model work remains open.

### Current-code qualifications for #51–53

For #51, lumped translation in absolute planar nodal coordinates admits a diagonal mass matrix. The present moving/spinning-hub generalized coordinates do not imply that the complete coupled mass matrix is diagonal. An alternative must retain hub reactions/torque, node rotational inertia carried by hub spin, gravity and whole-bike coupling. First receive equivalent contact-free force, energy and momentum controls, then loaded flat/sharp contacts. An embedded error estimate needs scaled position/velocity tolerances and event-aware acceptance; it is not an accuracy certificate across discontinuities. The [Bogacki–Shampine paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0893-9659(89)90079-7) has been located bibliographically, but its full text has not been read for this workstream. No new integrator or safe larger timestep is selected.

For #52, distinguish the collision witness from native force distribution. In the pinned MuJoCo source, [contact body weights/Jacobians](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/a892750516336d8358949f4518fcc4fbbd85beae/src/engine/engine_core_constraint.c#L1248) give a vertex contact (`con.vert >= 0`) one vertex body, while an element contact uses [normalized inverse-distance vertex weights](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/a892750516336d8358949f4518fcc4fbbd85beae/src/engine/engine_core_util.c#L574), with weighted body Jacobians evaluated at the common contact point. The blanket statement that all force goes to the deeper node is therefore not the general native law. The saved full-front/imported/coarse study contains 2,154 element-contact observations and the hardtail-rear/zero/finest study contains 2,456; neither contains a vertex contact. Those are observations across saved frames, not distinct events or every-step transition counts. Receive a minimal witness-switch fixture with actual contact fields, points, normals, weights and generalized forces before attributing a jump. Retain the nodal/contact-point moment discrepancy and signed work; a smoothing band changes mechanics and needs its own identity and energy/calibration gates.

For #53, the current casing has foundation and axial edge-extension elasticity/viscosity but no bending law. A useful high-frequency modal cutoff has not been demonstrated; this does not prove that every radial mode has the same frequency, since curvature, extension, prestress and contact can affect the spectrum. The [adaptive-wheel contract](ADAPTIVE_WHEEL.md) already requires persistent material identity and conservation on representation changes. Compare cheap tiers with a converged nodal reference on flat/rounded/sharp terrain, edge interiors, two supports, seams, unload/recontact and repeated impacts. Preserve mass, COM, momentum, work and unresolved-energy accounting through transfers. Flat-ground agreement alone cannot qualify a game tier on rocks.
