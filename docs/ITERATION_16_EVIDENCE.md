## Numerical receipt

The original nine moving-wheel tests still pass after the optional geometry-guard scratch reuse. Shared bike-tree extraction had separately passed the historical rig/browser checks before coupling.

Independent controls cover native mass and wheel spin energy; fixed/compliant rider and hardtail/full assembly; analytical initial linear/angular momentum with frame pitch rate, hub spin and displaced rider; separated flight; distributed contact-list force/axle-moment sums; corrected free-root and bearing reactions; full-suspension pivot moment; complete-wheel bearing/ground/gravity impulse balance; contact observation-cadence invariance; strict JSON replay; unsupported inputs and last-accepted-state rollback. Native diagnostic and integration acceleration/constraint force agree exactly in the recorded numerical cases.

## Separate refinement axes

For the 0.15 s full-suspension flat landing, changing only timestep gives:

| dt | Maximum energy residual | Final vertical momentum residual |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 31.25 microseconds | 0.218963 J | -0.0136191 kg m/s |
| 15.625 microseconds | 0.0847250 J | -0.00764298 kg m/s |
| 7.8125 microseconds | 0.0370978 J | -0.00384830 kg m/s |

The two finest final frame positions differ by about 6.1 micrometres. This demonstrates improvement for that bounded control; it does not establish universal timestep convergence for all custom setups.

At 15.625 microseconds over 0.4 s of the smooth bump, compare against 64 casing nodes and 32 terrain segments:

| Independent change | Final frame-position difference | Vertical ground-impulse difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Terrain 16 segments | 0.09497 mm | -0.02342 N s |
| Terrain 64 segments | 0.03663 mm | -0.00570 N s |
| Casing 128 nodes | 0.09369 mm | +0.05268 N s |

The 64-to-128-node peak-force change is about 1.27%. This short smooth-ground result does not close the earlier square-corner mesh problem or qualify pressure/stress. No representation is called ground truth on the basis of these comparisons.

A separate 0.03 s initially touching case reconstructs each wheel's momentum change from recorded bearing + ground + gravity forces. At fixed 31.25 microsecond integration, the largest component error is 0.1862 at 1 ms recording and 0.02725 at 0.125 ms (linear units kg m/s; angular kg m2/s). Intermediate recording intervals are not monotone. Changing integration timestep while retaining 0.125 ms observation gives a maximum component error of 0.05476 in the finest case. Thus sampled force quadrature is still limited and must not be advertised as converged impulse data. The test's explicit 0.06 component tolerance applies only to its named short control. Whole-system impulse/work are integrated every physics step and have separate convergence evidence above. A later high-rate load exporter or independent instantaneous Jacobian acceleration control should resolve the sampling floor before using bearing histories as precision dataset labels.

## Review and visual receipt

Primary-source research checked MuJoCo's flex/RNE omission, contact signs, node-axis projections, COM velocity origin and work terms. The complete-wheel correction deliberately excludes individual casing-node loads. Review fixes included valid assembly prefixes, complete mass partition, independent flex offsets, initial peak diagnostics, owned replay input, last-accepted-state rollback, null unevaluated geometry diagnostics, preset-label consistency and explicit fixed-rider guide semantics. No numerical limit was widened to make an unsupported dynamic run pass.

The browser suite exercises full/hardtail bump, separated flight, hard-landing termination, invalid initial travel, whole-record browser/headless comparison, saved replay, dirty state, cancel/restore, supersession, delayed import, timed playback, rapid pause/resume, lifecycle, hidden canvas recovery, 390/320 px layouts and all ten station tabs. Desktop full/hardtail and 320 px captures were inspected. Review prompted a tracking camera on narrow canvases so both tyres remain legible; desktop retains the whole recorded route. The first hosted browser attempt passed its physics/replay interactions and then exposed a test-harness URL-object error in the final navigation request; the request now passes its URL string. [PR #18 checks](https://github.com/CraigHutchinson/BikeLab/pull/18/checks) retain final browser artifacts and the exact-head CI receipt; the merged-main Pages workflow retains deployment evidence.

Not received: physical calibration, measured production-bike setup, complete collision/impact support, inflation/tread traction, brake/drive controls, articulated rider, 3D/finite width, layered soil/mud, scanned terrain ingestion, damage/pressure loss or real-time fidelity tiers.
