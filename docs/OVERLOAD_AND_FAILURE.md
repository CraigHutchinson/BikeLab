# Travel limits, overload and component failure

User requirement added to the iteration review: suspension travel must remain mechanically admissible. A severe impact must resolve through physical load paths, energy absorption and, when capacity is exceeded, component deformation or failure. It must not be represented by unlimited suspension travel, arbitrary tyre compression or an intact bicycle in an impossible configuration. Explicit examples include rim fracture/deformation, spoke breakage and tubeless air loss caused by rim deformation.

Status: required design/research and acceptance scope, not implemented damage physics. The present axial station stops at its travel-domain boundary. Its spring/damper force is not a prediction of bottom-out impact force or component strength. No numerical breaking thresholds are established by this document.

Ground shape is part of the causal load model: square rock edges, rounded obstacles and flat contact must be distinguished through spatial geometry, contact normals/locations and finite deformation behavior. [TERRAIN_CONTACT.md](TERRAIN_CONTACT.md) defines geometry inputs and adaptive detail/rate rules. Global axle force and one contact height alone cannot determine local rim/carcass/spoke stress. Adaptive computation must not miss impact/failure events or reset damage/pressure when returning to the cheaper model.

## Travel and impact contract

1. Derive extension/compression limits from the actual assembly, including fork/shock stroke, linkage limits and component clearances. Rear-wheel travel is derived from geometry; it is not interchangeable with shock stroke. Validate the limits across geometry adjustments and component swaps.
2. Distinguish free spring/damper travel, the engagement region of a compliant top-out/bottom-out element and the ultimate geometric constraint. Stop compression must consume specified available space; it must not add fictitious telescopic travel. Declare tolerances for constraint error.
3. Before continuing through an impact, implement and independently verify the chosen end-stop compliance/dissipation or constraint-impulse model. Resolve reaction loads through the actual attachment points, links, fork, frame, wheels and rider. An arbitrary position clamp or velocity reset does not account for momentum or energy.
4. A survivable bottom-out can compress the stop and transmit a large load without fracture. Loads beyond an evidenced failure envelope cause the appropriate damage/state transition; do not force a wheel to break just because suspension reaches full stroke. Conversely, a rim strike or lateral wheel overload can cause damage before suspension bottoms out.
5. Deformation after structural failure is a change to component geometry/constraints, not extra healthy suspension travel. If the model cannot represent the resulting state, end the run explicitly at the supported boundary. Never continue with impossible geometry or silently leave a failed component carrying its original loads.
6. Account separately for elastic recovery, damping, plastic deformation/fracture energy, contact impulses and external work. Damage must not create energy. Structural failure does not make mass, linear momentum or angular momentum disappear.

The current station already enforces 0 <= compression <= configured stroke at all integration trials/endpoints, restores the last valid state after a rejected step, and reports travel-limit. Retain that fallback until a verified stop/failure model can take over. It prevents unsupported output; it does not satisfy the eventual requirement to simulate the physical outcome of a severe impact.

## Component-by-component review basis

This inventory defines what must be investigated for each mechanism, not which failure must occur in a particular crash. Components without a supported model must be marked unsupported rather than implicitly indestructible. Each implemented assembly needs an explicit disposition for every applicable row and any added component.

| Component / interface | Loads and failure basis to establish | Modes and required consequences |
|---|---|---|
| Rim and bead seat | Local tyre/rim impact geometry, contact patch/obstacle shape, radial and lateral bending, hoop loading, material/section, spoke-hole and bead-seat stresses; permanent deformation and fracture criteria | Dent, buckle, crack/fracture or bead-seat distortion; update wheel/contact geometry and stiffness. Assess bead retention and seal integrity separately. Metal yielding and composite damage require different evidence. |
| Spokes and nipples | Individual installed tension, lacing and dish, load redistribution through rim/hub, tension range and fatigue, nipple/eyelet strength | Slackening is not breakage. Model tensile fracture, nipple pull-through or loosening separately; remove the failed load path, redistribute loads, and update wheel trueness/stiffness. A broken spoke must not retain its original stiffness. |
| Tyre casing, bead, tube/insert and tubeless seal | Pressure, casing/bead/rim compatibility, bead-seat geometry, lateral bead displacement, rim-strike deformation, local casing strain/contact and seal damage | Distinguish casing cut/pinch, bead unseating, transient burping and persistent leakage from rim/bed/tape/valve damage. Tube and tubeless modes differ. Inserts require their own consumed support/contact model. |
| Hub, flanges, bearings and axle/dropouts | Spoke forces, axle bending/shear, bearing reactions, drive/brake torque, clamp and dropout contact | Flange cracking, bearing damage, axle yield/fracture or wheel-retention failure; update alignment, joint/contact constraints and wheel attachment appropriately. |
| Fork assembly | Axial stop loads plus bending/torsion through stanchions, lowers, crown, steerer, bushings and axle attachments | Stop damage, binding, permanent bend, crown/steerer/lower fracture or mount failure. Failure follows local force/moment paths, not the axial spring force alone. |
| Shock, spring, damper and mounts | Spring stress, damper/shaft and seal loads, pressure where modelled, top/bottom-out loads, shaft buckling, eyelet/bushing/bolt stress and off-axis loading | Stop degradation, spring/shaft/mount failure, leakage or damping loss; distinguish changed force law from detachment. Internal hydraulic/cavitation models need separate evidence. |
| Frame, rear links, pivots and fasteners | Geometry-derived combined axial/bending/torsional loads, joint/contact stress, material/layup, buckling, fatigue and bolt preload/slip | Yield/buckle/crack, pivot or fastener failure; update geometry, compliance and constraint topology. Retain a truly rigid rear attachment for a healthy hardtail, not an artificial shock. |
| Headset, stem, handlebar and grips | Steering/braking/rider loads, bearing reactions, clamp torque/preload, bending/torsion and fatigue | Bearing/clamp slip, bar/stem/steerer damage or loss of rider grip/steering load path. Separate rider contact release from structural fracture. |
| Saddle, rails, clamp and seatpost/dropper | Rider load, extension and bending leverage, clamp/contact loads, fatigue and dropper support mechanism | Rail/post deformation/fracture, clamp slip or dropper collapse; update rider support/posture and the mechanical load path. |
| Cranks, pedals, bottom bracket and drivetrain | Pedal/ground-strike forces, crank bending, shaft/bearing loads, chain tension, torque, freewheel/engagement and fatigue | Pedal/crank/chain/gear/freehub failure or detachment; update force transmission and freewheel/drive state. Unsupported chain entanglement must stop rather than be improvised. |
| Brake rotor, caliper, mounts and hydraulics/cable | Brake torque/reaction, rotor bending/contact, mounting loads and thermal/hydraulic effects when consumed | Deformation/rubbing, mount failure, loss of actuation or fade; change actual braking/reaction behavior. Do not equate structural overload, friction saturation and thermal fade. |
| Test fixture and rider interfaces | Guide/restraint reactions, attachment capacity, hand/foot/saddle contact and declared support limits | Expose a fixture-capacity exceedance instead of attributing it to the bicycle. Rider release/contact limits need explicit rules; this is not a human-injury model. |

## Tubeless failure chain

Review the coupled path explicitly: impact/lateral loading -> rim or bead-seat deformation and/or spoke-load redistribution -> changed bead retention/seal gap -> air loss -> pressure-dependent tyre support and contact geometry -> changed loads and possible secondary damage. Not every dent or broken spoke causes deflation; the rim/tyre construction, deformation location and seal state determine whether leakage occurs.

Pressure must evolve through an evidenced leakage/thermodynamic model with bounded air mass and pressure, rather than becoming a generic damage multiplier. Record initial pressure, temperature assumptions, volume model, effective leakage parameters, sealant assumptions and the event triggering them. Instantaneous full deflation is only admissible as an explicitly justified limiting model. Following pressure loss, supported radial/tangential tyre behavior and bead/contact state must change consistently; a flat tyre cannot retain an unchanged inflated support law. Stop if that contact regime is unsupported.

## Evidence, parameters and configurability

There is no universal breaking force for a bicycle or even a rim. Capacity depends on load direction/application point, geometry/material, boundary conditions, load duration/rate, initial tension/preload, temperature where relevant, prior damage and fatigue history. Derive local stress/strain or a calibrated failure envelope from actual force/moment paths. Where a force/torque/energy threshold is a reduced model, state its fixture, load mode and applicability; force, impulse and impact energy are distinct quantities and cannot be interchanged without a model.

Before implementation, read primary component/manufacturer material, relevant test standards and actual test data. A published maximum service load or passing a standard test is not automatically the ultimate failure load. Record missing data explicitly. Use measured or justified conservative envelopes with uncertainty and held-out validation; illustrative thresholds remain labelled illustrative and cannot predict named-product survivability.

For each component/preset record: identity/revision/material/construction, dimensions, assembly/preload state, intact and damaged constitutive behavior, applicable failure modes, criterion variables with SI units, source/date/provenance, uncertainty, valid load/rate/cycle range, accumulated damage, and post-failure behavior or termination reason. Keep stock specification, calibrated failure data and editable experimental overrides distinct. Preserve all effective values and any stochastic seed in saved runs; never silently regenerate a stronger component when loading an old setup.

Expose enabled/unsupported failure models and their applicability. A diagnostic run with damage disabled must be labelled accordingly and cannot certify survival outside its valid envelope. Do not add inert failure controls until their physical effect is consumed.

## Acceptance gates before high-impact claims

- Stroke/clearance invariants under both top-out and bottom-out, including high speed, unequal wheel inputs and the hardest admitted fixture; no hidden overtravel or solver penetration beyond declared tolerance.
- Independent single-element stop references, impulse/momentum and energy budgets; refine timestep and event location before trusting impact peaks or damage triggers. One coarse numerical force spike must not decide fracture.
- Below-threshold survivable impacts and above-threshold failures for each implemented mode, plus combined loads and repeated/fatigue cases where claimed. Separate yield, loss of function and fracture.
- Rim strike and rim-deformation/seal tests, an intact-seal dent control case, spoke loss and load redistribution, and pressure decay/contact response. Include the possibility of damage without bottom-out and bottom-out without damage.
- End-to-end load-path tests: stop loads reach real mounts/frame/wheels; component failure changes those paths; no ghost support, hidden energy injection or implausible continuation. Unsupported post-failure states terminate visibly with the last valid evidence.
- Future 3D tests cover lateral wheel impacts, cornering plus braking, steering/rider loads and combined bending/torsion. A purely vertical break threshold does not establish turning-load capacity.
- Record component IDs, causal load/criterion, damage state, event time/uncertainty, pressure/seal state and topology changes. Browser/headless replay must reproduce events and clearly show damaged components or the reason the run stopped. Fit and validate on separate physical datasets.

## Delivery sequence

During this iteration's review, retain existing travel-domain enforcement and record the missing physical outcome as an open requirement. Next, before claiming high-impact capability, research and validate a bounded stop/load-path experiment and source-backed wheel/rim/spoke/seal cases with visual and headless consumers. Feed their attachment-load, deformation and damage-state requirements into the whole-bike solver decision now. Expand to the remaining component rows as their assemblies become active; do not postpone the entire overload contract to optional cosmetic damage after a playable trail.
