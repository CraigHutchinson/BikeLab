# Wheel assembly and geometric limits

`bikelab-wheel-limits-0.1` is a separate receiving experiment at `wheel-limits.html`. It locates a geometric rim boundary along a prescribed straight hub displacement and assesses declared tubeless assembly/maximum-pressure limits. It does not simulate impact, blowout, fracture, pressure loss or an inflated casing. The original footprint model and bicycle rig retain their existing numerical behaviour.

Run `node scripts/wheel-limits.mjs [input-or-record.json]`. Inputs are `{params, profile, assembly, delta}`. `params` uses the existing footprint SI settings; missing settings in a new setup use its defaults. `profile` uses the existing ordered solid-below polyline schema. `delta=[dx,dz]` is displacement in metres, bounded to a 2 m path. It is not velocity; path fraction is not time. A saved record requires complete geometry and assembly settings and is recomputed when loaded. CLI exits 0 for a clear path, 2 for any reached boundary/rejected assembly, and 1 for malformed input. A clear path does not certify a bike or assembly.

## Continuous rim boundary

The rim is a rigid circle. Its outer radius is independent of bead-seat diameter; the outer envelope cannot be smaller than the declared bead-seat radius. Neither dimension establishes rim wall thickness or strength. The tyre radius defines the authored-domain coverage envelope.

For hub `c(t)=c0+t d`, `0≤t≤1`, sweep the rim against the union of capsules around every terrain segment. For each vertex v, solve `|c0+t d−v|²=r²`. For a segment's unit normal n, solve `n·(c0+t d−a)=±r`, accepting only roots whose projection lies on the finite segment. Take the earliest admissible root, independently of display samples. Endpoint caps and finite-segment strips form the complete capsule; no point-count stiffness or impact law is involved. [PBRT sphere intersection](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Shapes/Spheres) is the quadratic-intersection reference. A cancellation-resistant quadratic root form and a relative discriminant-roundoff bound handle grazing. This is binary64 geometry with declared tolerances, not exact arithmetic.

Intersect the path with the interval that keeps the entire undeformed tyre envelope inside the authored horizontal domain. Stop at the earlier rim-contact/domain event; include all tied event types and all tied closest witnesses from the independent clearance query. Reaching the domain boundary exactly at the requested endpoint is recorded as a boundary. Stationary motion and motion inward from that boundary are distinct. Initial rim overlap, within-tolerance contact, hub burial and incomplete domain coverage are explicit outcomes. Starting in contact stops at fraction zero even if the proposed direction separates; no impact velocity is inferred.

Reports record fraction, travelled distance, event position, initial clearance, terminal clearance/witnesses and tolerances: 1e−10 path fraction, 1e−10 m initial-gap classification, and 32 machine epsilons relative to the quadratic discriminant terms. The inherited terrain query has its own 1e−10 m closest-feature tie tolerance. These are numerical tolerances, not physical cushioning or a casing thickness allowance.

The viewer clips its inspection slider to the accepted geometry. A static deformed tyre and loads are available only at the starting pose through the unchanged footprint solver. The rest of the approach shows the unloaded tyre reference envelope and rigid rim, without interpolating a deformed contour or inventing forces. The static model may refuse a sharp-corner pose before geometric rim contact; that refusal is distinct from contact and breakage. This experiment cannot be plugged directly into dynamic continuation without resolving tyre contact, impulse/work and events.

## Declared tubeless assembly assessment

Record tyre/rim identities, SI bead-seat diameters, nominal tyre width, rim inner width, hooked/hookless type, applicable tyre construction, tubeless/standards approval, width-pair/exact-pair approval, gauge pressure and each component maximum. Nominal tyre width uses whole-millimetre sizing expressed in metres; it is not a measured inflated width. A width ratio alone does not establish a compatible pair. Boolean approval values are supplied declarations, not independently verified manufacturer findings.

Each field can carry `{kind,reference}` provenance in `assembly.sources`, where kind is declared, illustrative, manufacturer or measured. Missing facts remain null/unknown. Supplied facts without explicit sources are labelled unverified declarations. Malformed/unknown fields and nonfinite or out-of-range values are rejected. Geometry is limited by the footprint schema; assembly dimensions and pressure have bounded SI validation.

For declared Schwalbe TLE/TLR 2020-onward hookless assemblies, the [manufacturer hookless PDF](https://www.schwalbe.com/media/1f/82/33/1706602649/Hookless_Info_EN.pdf), pp. 1–2, supplies nominal width bands with maximum service-pressure recommendations. Relevant MTB bands are 45–54 mm at 300 kPa, 55–64 mm at 250 kPa and 65–74 mm at 200 kPa. The module retains the full published 18–84 mm table and source/date/scope; it does not extrapolate beyond it or apply it to another brand/hooked construction. Lower declared tyre or rim maxima still govern. Missing exact limits/compatibility remain unknown even when a generic band is known. The separate [Schwalbe hookless explanation](https://www.schwalbe.com/en/technology-faq/hookless/) discusses 5 bar for road use; this is not a universal MTB allowance.

Assessment states are `incompatible`, `unknown` or `within-declared-limits`. A known mismatch or known pressure exceedance takes precedence over missing evidence and blocks both path advancement and the static load report. Zero gauge pressure also blocks continuation because a supported deflated tyre model is absent. Unknown evidence permits geometry inspection only. Within-declared-limits is limited to the checked declarations and maximum-pressure bounds: minimum pressure, load, temperature, fit tolerances, construction validity and physical calibration can still be unresolved. None of these states certifies survivability or turns the radial foundation coefficient into a pressure-dependent law.

The demonstration pair is explicitly synthetic: a 60-584 tyre and 584 × 30 hookless rim, a 305 mm outer rim envelope, invented component maxima and approvals. It is not a product preset. Its width-band recommendation is the sourced part. Editing pressure changes admission; it does not silently scale tyre stiffness. Saved declarations and their sources travel with the record.

## Blowout and breakage prerequisites

Every result explicitly retains null criteria for burst pressure, bead release, casing cut, rim yield/fracture, spoke fracture/fatigue and leakage area. Manufacturer operating maxima, spoke pretension, inspection criteria and standard-test passes are not ultimate strength measurements. Rim contact is not automatically a puncture or fracture; suspension bottom-out is not automatically wheel failure.

The eventual causal chain remains: local impact/lateral load and component deformation → supported casing/bead/rim/spoke criterion → changed topology or seal opening → air-mass/pressure evolution → changed tyre support/contact → redistributed loads. Store component construction, local loads, uncertainty, prior damage and event location. Plastic/fracture work, mass and momentum must remain accounted for. End at the supported boundary if the resulting state is unavailable; do not preserve ghost stiffness after breakage or inflated support after deflation.

Sharp-feature forces require tangential casing freedom and/or an independently justified feature-contact formulation. Scalar radial balance cannot uniquely assign several corner normals. At a convex obstacle corner, exterior space is a union of face-exterior regions; enforcing every face's exterior inequality at once wrongly takes their intersection. Feature-aligned remeshing also needs material-coordinate/energy consistency. These remain prerequisites before dynamic wheel impacts, whole-bike coupling, layered soil/mud and finite-width 3D turning.
