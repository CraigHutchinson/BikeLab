# Seated and unseated rider follow-up

User requirement, captured 2026-10-21: support seated riding and riding clear of the saddle, including a bent-leg hovering posture in which bike and limb motion can absorb terrain while rider centre-of-mass motion is smaller. Riding style, intent and anticipation matter. This is a researched follow-up after passive D5a, not an implemented controller or a claim that the COM stays fixed.

## Prior art and its limits

GPT-6.1 Sol gathered the primary references below; the field-load, acceleration, muscle-activity and skill papers were also checked against their full text during consolidation. These small and task-specific studies motivate observables and experiments. They do not identify a general MTB rider controller, universal when-to-stand threshold or calibrated joint coefficients for BikeLab.

## Mechanical and control contract

The following are BikeLab engineering decisions to investigate, rather than coefficients or laws established by those studies:

- Represent seated support, unseated hovering/attack posture and transitions. Unseated does not mean fully extended legs. Desired posture/strategy and actual saddle support are separate recorded state: intending to sit does not guarantee contact, and a bump may unload a seated rider.
- Give the saddle unilateral compressive support, clearance, separation and recontact. Declare saddle shape, contact compliance/friction, pelvis contact geometry and frame-relative seat height. Start with a fixed seat height; a moving dropper needs its own state and work contract. Do not weld or teleport the pelvis to switch modes.
- Let arms/legs and bike suspension share the response. Track whole-rider COM separately from hip/trunk position and combined bike/rider COM. A relatively stable rider COM can be an observed result or declared control objective; it must not become an exact world-height constraint or an infinite-force target.
- Separate passive joint mechanics from bounded active posture control. Absorbing terrain, resisting/pumping, maintaining traction and choosing comfort/speed can require different commands. Record intent, feedback, anticipation horizon and commands; do not infer a unique style from terrain alone. Controller gains, posture ranges and transition policies remain uncalibrated until identified against appropriate measurements.
- Account for active joint torque, speed, power and signed work, including changing rest angles or stiffness. Preserve whole-system impulse/momentum and native load ownership. Energy injected by a posture command must not masquerade as suspension recovery or free pump gain.
- Keep hand/foot/saddle loads separate, at their actual application points. The current paired-limb planar model and bilateral foot closure are a retained-foot surrogate: they cannot validate separate lead/trail-leg motion, ankle/crank effects, flat-pedal release, grip release or lateral balance. Future reduction/LOD must declare those omissions.
- Choose each rider tier's frequency/motion range from receiving data. Five rigid groups are not evidence for soft-tissue or high-frequency body vibration fidelity. Compare cheaper real-time approximations against a calibrated richer reference with matched inputs and observable errors; conserve mass, momentum, stored energy and controller state when switching tiers.

## Ordered follow-up and acceptance

| Stage | Receiving experiment | Required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1: passive support | Existing Trail bike, both hardtail and full suspension: unseated posture, seated equilibrium, saddle unloading and recontact | Actual segment/attachment rendering, contact states and loads; matched locked-posture control; guarded domains/rollback; signed interface work; separate physical momentum residual and numerical closure contribution. Follow the [D5a handoff](ITERATION_20_HANDOFF.md). |
| S2: data and identification | Obtain a usable primary dataset or reproduce a published protocol, with repeated seated/hovering/absorption trials | Synchronized terrain, speed, bike/tyre/suspension setup, saddle height, body geometry/mass, segment motion and hand/pedal/saddle loads. Record rider instructions, skill/style and between-rider/trial variation. Establish sensor frames, timing, bandwidth, uncertainty and data reuse rights before fitting. |
| S3: bounded active absorption | Same obstacle/entry state, compare passive, chosen absorbing and chosen resisting/pumping commands | Joint trajectories/efforts/work, rider and bike COM, load peaks/impulses, wheel contact and suspension/tyre travel. Compare events at matched trail position, plus frequency-dependent response/phase where measured. An acceleration reduction alone does not prove correct COM motion or energy dissipation. |
| S4: repeatable strategy and game tier | Recorded intent and controls drive the same headless/browser engine; later adaptive style and anticipation | Reproducible transitions; timestep/contact/refinement and observation invariance; independent validation trials; saturation/latency/unsupported cases; target-hardware cost and tier error. Preserve [manoeuvre gates](RIDER_MANOEUVRES.md) and later 3D scope. |

Declare numerical and physical acceptance budgets before fitting. Distinguish illustrative defaults, numerically verified mechanics, measured calibration and player preference. No source reviewed here supplies a universal seat-toggle policy, active joint gains or a guaranteed stable COM trajectory. Research and dataset acquisition remain explicit work; this note adds no public configuration knobs or implemented riding modes.
