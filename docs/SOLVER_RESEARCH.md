## Scope and evidence rules

Compare three credible routes to the next visible hardtail/full-suspension rig while preserving an eventual spatial bicycle. The reference counts remain 6/7 planar and 10/11 spatial independent coordinates with a fixed rider and single-pivot rear mechanism. External fixtures, tyre internal states and actual multi-link loop constraints must be counted separately. Quaternion components are not independent coordinates.

Engine capability is not a calibrated bicycle model. None of the documents below establishes wet forest-soil parameters, finite mud-cover/buried-root load transfer, bicycle carcass/bead/rim stress, spoke fracture, leakage or held-out riding fidelity. A documented deformable body is a possible mechanism substrate, not evidence that those laws are supplied. Travel must stay inside assembly geometry, with explicit termination at unsupported impact or damage states.

Primary pages were read on the date above. Rapier's unversioned pages displayed JavaScript 0.21 and Rust 0.36; MuJoCo numerical pages below are version 3.3.7, while its official WASM README is moving `main`; Chrono references are labelled 10.0.0. **These are documentation observations, not a mutually compatible pinned dependency set.** Resolve source SHA, matching bindings/core, precision, features, licence and availability before implementation. Do not infer an installed release from a live documentation sidebar.

The candidate grouping denotes engine families, not one interchangeable numerical method: each has solver/integrator/contact choices that must be part of its experiment identity. Do not compare defaults and attribute every difference to engine quality.

### Primary source details

- **R1:** [Rapier joints](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/joints/) lists the relevant joint types and insertion APIs.
- **R2:** [Rapier joint constraints](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/joint_constraints/) distinguishes reduced coordinates from impulse constraints, tree restrictions, combined loop closure and availability of joint forces. Evaluate both representations on the same small assembly if reaction observability cannot be resolved without changing formulation.
- **R3:** [Rapier soft bodies](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/soft_bodies/) describes soft frames attached to joints, deformable colliders, shape matching, constraint and FEM approaches, and plasticity/tearing. Those features do not establish a pressurised anisotropic bicycle tyre or a soil constitutive law.
- **R4:** [Rapier JavaScript determinism](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/determinism/) requires identical initial values, construction/removal order and version; platform-dependent `Math.sin`/`Math.cos` initialization can defeat the guarantee. The guide suggests snapshot hashes for repeatability. That guarantee does not automatically include BikeLab's future custom contact/soil code.
- **R5:** [Rapier Rust determinism](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/determinism/) distinguishes local determinism from `enhanced-determinism`, specifies floating-point/initialisation requirements and a `simd8` incompatibility. Test the exact build rather than assuming the JavaScript guarantee applies to arbitrary native features.
- **M1:** [MuJoCo 3.3.7 overview, equality/flex](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/3.3.7/overview.html#equality) documents loop constraints and deformable elements with contact. Independent coordinates and numerical constraint residuals need separate inspection.
- **M2:** [MuJoCo 3.3.7 modelling](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/3.3.7/modeling.html#solver-parameters) explains impedance/reference parameters and the soft-constraint model. Treat this as a compliance/error-budget issue, not permission to extend healthy fork/shock geometry.
- **M3:** [Official MuJoCo WASM README](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/main/wasm/README.md) describes canonical upstream bindings, packaged WASM, threading headers and manual handle lifetime. Copied contact objects become stale after stepping; recorded diagnostics must use current data. Its platform cautions and moving branch need checking against the selected artifact.
- **C1:** [Chrono 10.0 introduction](https://api.projectchrono.org/10.0.0/introduction_chrono.html) supplies the multibody/contact/integrator and native build capability inventory. Selection of a particular NSC/SMC system, integrator and tolerance remains a later source/API review.
- **C2:** [Chrono 10.0 tyre subsystem](https://api.projectchrono.org/10.0.0/group__vehicle__wheeled__tire.html) lists ANCF, co-rotational FEA, shell and rigid tyre classes. It supplies no bicycle calibration receipt.
- **C3:** [Chrono 10.0 terrain models](https://api.projectchrono.org/10.0.0/vehicle_terrain.html) distinguishes terrain formulations and compatible tyre families. Its near-real-time language is not a BikeLab measurement. Moving patches or a soil-parameter callback alone do not prove persistent finite-layer/root coupling.

## Recommended minimal comparison experiment

### Immediate receiving prerequisite: clearance geometry

The receiving prerequisite is a visible/headless rigid-circle versus ordered-polyline clearance probe, alongside the existing wheel station: authored SI profiles with provenance, flat/rounded/explicit vertical-face controls, nearest witnesses, normals and signed gap. It supplies common geometry/reference data for later candidate trials; it selects no engine and applies no forces, stresses or dynamics. The continuation implements this bounded fixture; see [the probe contract](TERRAIN_PROBE.md) and iteration 4 evidence for its verification status. Dynamic candidate trials below remain proposed.

Read primary [Eberly, Distance Between Point and Line, Ray, or Line Segment](https://www.geometrictools.com/Documentation/DistancePointLine.pdf), discussion on page 2. For centre `C` and nondegenerate segment `A,B`, set `M=B-A`, `t=clamp(dot(M,C-A)/dot(M,M),0,1)` and `Q=A+t*M`; the nearest segment distance is `d=norm(C-Q)`. BikeLab's explicit extension for an admitted exterior centre and radius `R` is `g=d-R`, `n=(C-Q)/d`, wheel witness `C-R*n`. Thus wheel witness minus terrain witness equals `g*n`: positive gap is clearance and negative gap is overlap. These gap/sign choices are BikeLab conventions, not a soil/contact constitutive model.

Validate ordered nondecreasing x, explicit vertical segments, finite nonduplicate endpoints, solid-below interpretation, full horizontal footprint inside the declared domain and exterior centre before evaluation. Reject buried/on-boundary centres or zero distance explicitly. Nearest boundary distance alone cannot classify inside/outside. For left-to-right terrain the segment outward normal is `(-dz,dx)/length`, but a nearest-vertex radial normal is generally different. Preserve ties and feature IDs or define a deterministic selection; shared vertices must not become duplicated future forces. Verify analytic plane/slope/corner witnesses and signs, segment-subdivision invariance, finite-radius/rounded-polyline refinement, vertical-front-face contact before the centre reaches the top, and browser/headless output. A rigid clearance probe establishes none of the dynamic gates below.

Recommend a **two-stage comparison**, not engine selection: first one identical small mechanical problem in Rapier and MuJoCo, with Chrono as a native reference/candidate if its headless build and trace export are available without a browser port. Do not require a full tyre/soil implementation or general plugin architecture to start. Browser parity must remain a gate before any candidate becomes the live browser solver. A native-only reference may remain useful without qualifying for that role.

### A. Geometry, constraints and stops

Create an immutable synthetic fixture specification shared by adapters: SI, x forward/y left/z up; named bodies, positive-definite inertias, attachment frames, spring/damper signs, geometry-derived stroke, identical initial state and input schedule. No manufacturer accuracy claim. Audit the 6/7 planar counts and fixture-reduced 3/4 counts; build the 10/11 spatial tree and check an off-axis force/moment response without steering control. Add one small closed-loop linkage fixture so a single-pivot success cannot conceal future loop drift.

Run independent cases: gravity-free free rigid-body momentum, static axle-load/moment balance, interior-travel oscillator matching the existing independent reference where its assumptions apply, a geometry-only linkage sweep, and compression/rebound into a declared compliant stop with an ultimate travel boundary. Stop unsupported continuation and retain the last valid state. Rejection must restore warm starts, constitutive history and all other solver state, not only positions. Root-locate/refine event time or conservatively reject an interval when a within-step excursion cannot be excluded; endpoint checks alone are inadequate.

For a frictionless one-coordinate stop-impact control use independently derived impulse/momentum and the declared restitution or compliance energy budget. Do not compare a rigid impulse model's instantaneous peak force with a compliant contact peak. For the real bike, instrument attachment wrenches or explicitly identify unavailable channels; inferring every internal overload from one axle load is unacceptable.

### B. Spatial contact and extension seam

Use a wheel-envelope contact fixture over plane, ramp, circular arc, finite-radius corner and a vertical-front-face obstacle, plus two simultaneous supports. Store actual geometry, edge radius/margins, normals, application points and feature IDs. Start with frictionless known geometry and one declared shared compliance model, so engine defaults do not introduce unequal tyre stiffness. Disable duplicate engine support where externally owned contact supplies it. Include grazing contact, front-face contact before the axle reaches the top, separation/recontact and an off-centre 3D force/moment check. Rigid wheel controls establish geometry only, not carcass stress or handling.

Exercise a tiny synthetic stateful cover/substrate interface under quasi-static loading, unloading and save/restart. It may explicitly report unsupported rather than invent soil mechanics. A useful later adapter must return equal/opposite integrated wrenches and work, preserve cover state and route load once through cover to root; independently varying tyre and soil compliance must remain observable. Bare-root and homogeneous-soil controls are mandatory before claiming the covered-root gate. No detailed soil rankings, failure threshold or full layered model is selected by this experiment.

### Preregistered comparison gates and output

These are proposed synthetic software gates, to fix in the experiment specification before observing results. They are not physical calibration tolerances:

| Observable | Proposed gate |
|---|---|

Record engine/core/binding SHA, compiler/features/precision, timestep, iteration/residual settings, collision margins, complete initial state/input/geometry hashes, state serialisation coverage, event brackets, contact impulses/wrenches, work/energy and explicit unavailable diagnostics. Test uninterrupted versus snapshot/restart execution, including contact warm starts and application-owned persistent state. A screenshot comparison cannot establish replay equivalence.

Deliver one small browser replay/overlay receiving those traces and one headless report, following the existing comparison sidecar pattern. A reference must never correct candidate forces implicitly. Mark contact-force versus time-averaged impulse channels clearly. Keep current station records compatible.

Do not benchmark during this research. Once correctness and browser/native feasibility pass, measure serially on named hardware with the actual coupled workload, including repeated/two-wheel impacts; report median/tail/worst observed solve costs and backlog separately from rendering. A worker's offline solve/replay timing cannot establish a live deadline. Adaptive or multirate execution follows an always-detailed converged reference, with event prediction, impulse/work synchronization and state-transfer tests from TERRAIN_CONTACT.md.

## Decision left open

Rapier merits an early feasibility attempt because its documented browser determinism and dual joint formulations directly address current boundaries. MuJoCo is an equally necessary comparison because official WASM and articulated/deformable mechanics now provide a credible alternative, with a clear strict-limit risk. Chrono merits a bounded native reference/extension check because its tyre/terrain breadth directly addresses future requirements; browser delivery remains unresolved. This ordering is a research-cost recommendation, not a performance or fidelity ranking. Choose only after the geometry, reaction-observability, strict-travel, restart and browser/headless evidence above exists.
